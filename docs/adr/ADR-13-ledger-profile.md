# ADR-13: Household property ledger profile

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-19
- **Related:** [domain model](../domain-model.md), [ADR-07](ADR-07-monthly-cashflow.md)
- **Test vectors:** [L-01–L-08](../test-vectors.md#ledger-vectors)

## Context
The initial accounting model supports a household/personal owner of property, not a business accounting or tax system.

## Decision
Use double-entry accounting and the account types assets, liabilities, equity, income, and expenses. The focused chart covers in-scope cash, property reference value, mortgage and other included liabilities, opening equity, rental income, and property operating expenses (interest, tax, insurance, maintenance/repairs, utilities). Debit increases assets/expenses; credit increases liabilities/equity/income. Contra asset and contra expense accounts reverse those normal balance directions. Account type determines normal balance and validation; `AccountCode` is a separate readable code for grouping/order and never determines posting behavior. Use broad code ranges by statement class: 1000–1999 assets, 2000–2999 liabilities, 3000–3999 equity, 4000–4999 income, and 5000–5999 expenses. Within assets, order accounts by liquidity; within liabilities, order by expected settlement timing where applicable. Leave gaps for additions and do not renumber existing codes when adding a leaf account. This is a project convention, not a GAAP-mandated numbering scheme.

In the implementation, `AccountCategory` represents the five classes and `BalanceSide` represents debit or credit. `AccountCategory::normal_balance(contra)` derives debit for asset/expense and credit for liability/equity/income; a contra flag reverses only asset and expense. `AccountCode` is an opaque numeric value accepting only 1000–5999, displays as four digits, sorts numerically, and must fall within the category's range. An `Account` has a caller-supplied opaque `AccountId`, code, category, contra flag, and non-empty name. A `ChartOfAccounts` rejects duplicate IDs and duplicate codes. These checks are local to one chart; they do not define posting or opening-balance rules. `CashFlowCategory` is a separate purpose classification (`Operating`, `Investing`, `Financing`) to be supplied for cash-flow components/postings, not stored on an account. Thus a cash account can participate in all three classifications depending on the transaction component.

Unit tests alongside the accounting modules cover these representation rules; the shared L-01–L-08 vectors cover posting and projection behavior. L-07 is a generic Ledger accrual/projection edge, not a mortgage calculation convention; mortgage behavior follows ADR-14.

Opening assets and liabilities are supplied facts; opening equity is the balancing net worth, not a simulated-period posting. If the user supplies net worth, it must equal opening assets less opening liabilities. Negative net worth is valid when balances reconcile; it does not stop a run. Store opening balances as opening facts, not generated pre-start or period transactions. The initial domain activities recognize and settle their monthly interest in the same monthly close; L-07 separately checks that the generic Ledger projection can represent an accrual with only partial cash settlement. That capability does not make unpaid interest part of the ADR-14 mortgage convention.

Opening-balance records contain an account ID, debit/credit side, and non-negative amount at the currency's minor-unit precision. Only asset, liability, and equity accounts may carry an opening balance; omitted accounts are zero. Ordinary non-cash assets cannot carry an abnormal credit balance. A cash-position account is explicitly designated in the chart and may have either a debit or credit balance at opening or month-end; its signed balance is the modeled property-related cash position. A credit balance is not an inferred overdraft, loan, payable, or funding event. The model records only the in-scope property activity and does not claim to represent the household's complete finances. Transactions committed together for a month are validated against that month's combined ending balance, so intermediate transaction order does not determine validity. Contra assets may carry their normal credit balance and reduce the asset total. A validated opening set uses one currency, has unique known account IDs, and balances total debits to total credits. Debit equity balances represent negative equity/net worth. Asset, liability, equity, and net-worth totals are derived from account categories and sides, with the cash-position account contributing its signed amount to modeled assets. Optional user-declared net worth is checked against derived assets less liabilities; the equity balance remains part of the balancing opening facts. These records create no period transaction or cash-flow component.

The Ledger must not turn an allowed negative property cash-flow outcome into a runtime failure. A cash-position account may carry a credit balance, reported as a negative signed cash position; the balanced counterpart remains the actual in-scope expense, asset, liability, or equity posting supplied by the activity. Do not create a balancing loan, overdraft, owner contribution, or other unprovided economic event. This representation permits the scoped ledger and its projections to reconcile without modeling household solvency, delinquency, or funding behavior.

The initial cash-flow projection uses the direct method: derive actual in-scope property cash receipts/payments from explicitly designated cash-position account IDs, classifying each cash posting as operating, investing, or financing. A combined cash account may carry different classifications across postings. Each cash posting in the requested period must have a category; non-cash postings must not. Missing or misplaced classification is an input/proposal validation error, not a late projection failure. Rent received and property operating expenses (including interest paid) are operating; property purchases/sales are investing; loan principal is financing. Owner contributions are included only when explicitly supplied as an in-scope activity. Non-cash effects do not enter the direct cash-flow projection. This is a scoped simulation presentation convention, not a complete household statement or statutory reporting advice.

M1 projection APIs use an inclusive period. Balance sheet is as of its end month (including that month's postings); its account detail contains only asset, liability, and equity accounts. Income and direct cash flow cover both endpoints. Balance sheet current equity includes cumulative net income since ledger opening; no closing entry is generated. Cash-account selection is caller supplied, must be non-empty, and each selected account must be explicitly marked as a non-contra cash-position asset; codes are never used to infer cash accounts. Projection range, account selection, and cash classifications are validated before statement calculations begin.

## Alternatives considered
- Corporate chart, depreciation, payroll, or tax accounting: outside personal-property scope.
- Plugging opening differences to miscellaneous income: misstates period performance.
- Indirect cash flow as the initial projection: requires reconciliation adjustments for non-cash and timing differences that are not part of the initial model.

## Consequences
Balances and statements derive from opening balances and posted entries. Principal repayment reduces the designated cash position and loan liability, not expense; interest is recognized separately as an expense. Negative equity is represented by a debit balance in equity. A credit cash-position balance is shown as a negative signed cash amount and does not imply a separate financing event or liability. The ledger is a scoped property model, not a complete household balance sheet. Future accounting profiles require an explicit design decision.
